\section{Related Work}
\label{sec:related}

\subsection{Online trajectory generation and complete target states}

OTG generates the next feasible motion state during execution, allowing a robot to react to new targets without completing an offline plan. Kr{"o}ger and Wahl formalized this control-cycle view and classified algorithms by the highest constrained and nonzero derivative~\cite{kroeger2010otg,kroeger2010book}. Reflexxes exposed these ideas as reusable motion libraries~\cite{kroeger2011reflexxes}. Earlier single- and multi-axis methods considered jerk-bounded or soft motion profiles with different restrictions on initial and terminal acceleration~\cite{macfarlane2003jerk,broquere2008soft,haschke2008online}. Ruckig extended this line to arbitrary complete target states under velocity, acceleration, and jerk constraints, including nonzero terminal acceleration and multi-axis synchronization~\cite{berscheid2021ruckig}.

These works establish why derivative targets are meaningful inputs. They do not, by themselves, analyze what happens when a moving position stream is repeatedly paired with zero target derivatives at a fixed control period. In particular, correctness of a solver for the state it receives does not imply that the application supplied a state consistent with its intended motion. Our use of \termismatch{} concerns this interface semantics. We do not propose a new general OTG algorithm, and we do not treat an implementation that exactly satisfies $(p^\star,0,0)$ as malfunctioning.

Jerk-limited industrial motion has also been studied as smooth waypoint blending and path-accurate online generation~\cite{macfarlane2003jerk,lange2016path,biagiotti2008trajectory}. Those methods motivate limiting jerk to control excitation and mechanical stress. Our result is compatible with that motivation: each P-only pulse is itself jerk bounded. The artifact arises because a smooth stop is requested too often, not because jerk continuity is absent.

\subsection{Path parameterization versus receding target generation}

Time-optimal path parameterization assigns a time law to a prescribed geometric path. Classical work considered manipulator dynamics and path constraints~\cite{bobrow1985time,shin1985minimum,shiller1989path}; later convex and robust formulations improved numerical treatment~\cite{verscheure2009time,pham2014topp}. Acceleration-bounded path following and waypoint blending have also been demonstrated on real manipulators~\cite{kunz2012time}. These formulations differ from the present receding problem. Here, the input is a sequence of state targets, the generator is restarted from its previous output each tick, and the target derivative fields can redefine the task at every restart.

The distinction prevents two category errors. First, a rest-to-rest reachability boundary does not claim global time optimality along a robot path. Second, a fix to the target-state contract is not a new path-planning or path-parameterization algorithm. The single-axis derivation is instead a local diagnostic for a recurring interface pattern. Multi-axis synchronization, coupled dynamics, Cartesian singularities, and path fidelity remain outside the theorem.

Several OTG variants emphasize time synchronization and reaction from arbitrary states~\cite{ahn2004aspot,beul2016multirotor,wang2020synchronous}. They reinforce that terminal derivatives affect the admissible profile family. Our contribution is to make one consequence explicit and dimensionless: when a target asks for rest after a displacement $\abs{\vref}T$, the ratio of that displacement to the maximum one-period rest-to-rest displacement predicts whether a per-tick stop is reachable.

\subsection{Reference management and preview}

Reference and command governors modify requested inputs so that a pre-stabilized system respects constraints. Predictive reference management and generalized reference governors supply a mature language for admissible commands~\cite{bemporad1998reference,gilbert2002governor}; a broad survey connects them to model predictive control and input shaping~\cite{garone2017governors}. Our scheduled-target contract is related in spirit because it distinguishes a desired reference from an executable state target. It is not a reference-governor stability result: there is no closed-loop plant model here, and no invariant admissible set for disturbances is proved.

Preview alone also does not resolve state semantics. Position lookahead can move the endpoint farther away, and a minimum-duration setting can prevent early completion, but neither supplies the terminal derivative required by the intended invariant motion. This observation motivates the E16 interventions. We compare matched velocity against tested lookahead horizons and duration settings while auditing the full within-period profile, rather than assuming that any form of preview is equivalent.\claimtag{C4}

\subsection{Causal derivative estimation}

Constructing velocity from positions is a numerical differentiation problem. Finite differences are exact for low-order polynomials on their assumed grids but amplify measurement noise. Savitzky--Golay filtering interprets smoothing and differentiation as local polynomial least squares~\cite{savitzky1964smoothing,gorry1990least}; regularized differentiation addresses noisy and nonsmooth signals through an explicit fidelity--variation trade-off~\cite{chartrand2011differentiation}. State-space filters instead combine a motion model with stochastic assumptions~\cite{kalman1960filter,brown2012filtering}.

No estimator is uniformly best across delay, noise, dropout, and irregular sampling. Accordingly, we separate two claims. The algebraic \FutureOOne{} construction is causal and affine-exact under a fixed-grid scheduled-position contract. Robustness of a particular observer is empirical and limited to its tested envelope. E17 selects a local-polynomial observer using development data before evaluating a frozen synthetic holdout; A04 independently selects \FutureOOne{} for the recorded fixed-grid case. The different selections are intentional evidence that the matched velocity target is the remedy while the observer remains contract- and domain-specific. The failed irregular-timestamp replay is therefore informative rather than contradictory.\claimtag{C5}\claimtag{C6}\claimtag{C13}

The gap addressed by this paper lies at the intersection of these literatures. Complete-state OTG makes target derivatives enforceable, but the repeated zero-derivative contract has not been reduced to a one-period dimensionless boundary and then isolated with causal, exact-profile interventions. That combination---reachability analysis, target-state semantics, and bounded observer evidence---is the paper's intended novelty.
