\subsection{Derivative accuracy does not remove availability delay}
\label{sec:results-derivatives}

% CLAIM: C05
\Cref{fig:derivative-timing} summarizes the information required by the three
finite-difference stencils, while \cref{tab:derivative-accuracy} reports their
errors against analytic truth.  Across the three references, offline centered
velocity estimates reduce derivative RMSE by factors from
\CenteredVelocityAccuracyFactorMin{} to
\CenteredVelocityAccuracyFactorMax{} relative to the corresponding backward
differences; acceleration factors range from
\CenteredAccelerationAccuracyFactorMin{} to
\CenteredAccelerationAccuracyFactorMax{}.  Those accuracy gains do not make
the offline estimate causal: its value represented at \(t_k\) requires
\(p_{k+1}\).  The causal centered construction instead carries one sample of
native delay before propagation.  These are derivative-source properties, not
position-following lags.

\subsection{Reliable velocity helps under the analytic information condition}
\label{sec:results-analytic}

% CLAIM: C03
On every smooth analytic reference, replacing P with PV truth reduces both
position RMSE and best grid lag
(\cref{fig:phase-a-ablation,tab:analytic-tracking}).  The RMSE reduction ranges from
\AnalyticVelocityImprovementMin\% to
\AnalyticVelocityImprovementMax\%.  P-only best lags span
\AnalyticPOnlyLagMinMS--\AnalyticPOnlyLagMaxMS{} ms, whereas PV truth records
\AnalyticPVLagMS{} ms on all three references.  The paired absolute RMSE
values, rather than the relative percentages alone, are given in
\cref{tab:analytic-tracking}.  This supports the value of reliable target
velocity for these three clean, pre-specified references and this fixed
follower protocol.  It does not show that an online position-only estimator
will supply velocity of comparable quality.

% CLAIM: C04
Adding analytic acceleration does not improve the recorded position result
over PV truth.  PV- and PVA-truth RMSE values differ by at most
\(\AnalyticPVPVAMaxRMSEDifference\,\si{\radian}\), and both have the same
\AnalyticPVLagMS{}-ms best lag on every reference.  Mechanistically, both
conditions reach the same requested endpoint position and velocity; the
different endpoint acceleration does not change the recorded position
endpoint in these runs.  This numerical non-result is not an equivalence
claim and provides no evidence about acceleration-estimator quality, internal
profile quality, control effort, or acceleration-active reference regimes.

\subsection{The next-cycle oracle validates the index convention}

% CLAIM: C08
Supplying the analytic state that belongs to the next output time gives
\OracleLagMS{}-ms best grid lag on all three references, with worst RMSE
\SI{\OracleWorstRMSE}{\radian} and one-step reachability recorded for every evaluated
target.  This near-zero-error result is a protocol sanity control: it isolates
correct future-state timing from estimation and confirms the implemented
\(\mathrm{target}[k]\mapsto\mathrm{output}[k+1]\) convention on these feasible
references.  It consumes one future sample, is noncausal, and is neither a
deployable predictor nor a general tracking-error bound.

\subsection{Finite-difference pipelines do not improve the development trace}
\label{sec:results-csv}

% CLAIM: C06,N01
The P-only condition records \SI{\CSVPOnlyRMSE}{\radian} RMSE and
\CSVPOnlyLagMS{} ms best lag on the fixed-grid development trace
(\cref{fig:csv-negative,tab:csv-tracking}).  Every tested unfiltered
finite-difference-derived pipeline has larger RMSE, spanning
\SI{\CSVDifferenceRMSEMin}{\radian}--\SI{\CSVDifferenceRMSEMax}{\radian};
their best lags span
\CSVDifferenceLagMinMS--\CSVDifferenceLagMaxMS{} ms.  The observation is about
the complete pipeline, including the protocol's fixed admissibility projection
when triggered and subsequent \OrdinaryRuckig{} following.  It is one
development trace with no derivative truth, so it cannot support
generalization to independent trajectories or show that derivative
information is intrinsically unhelpful.

% CLAIM: C07
P and all PV rows require no target projection, whereas every PVA
finite-difference row projects \CSVProjectionRate\% of requested targets.  The
raw differentiated acceleration peak is
\SI{\CSVRawAccelerationPeak}{\radian\per\second\squared}, approximately
\CSVAccelerationLimitMultiple{} times the configured acceleration limit.
These values diagnose the pre-projection target estimates, not a measured
robot, simulated plant, or executed-command acceleration violation.  Because
projection is absent from P/PV but active for all PVA rows, the design cannot
separate estimator error from the projection intervention.

\subsection{Frozen direct-command audit and measured runtime}
\label{sec:results-v3}

% CLAIM: C10
Within the frozen synthetic v3 protocol, the direct method records zero
continuous V/A/internal-J violations, zero projections, and zero fallbacks
over \VThreeDirectCycleCount{} command cycles
(\cref{fig:v3-direct,tab:v3-safety}).  All \VThreeDirectCycleCount{} command
states are point-admissible and one-step reachable, and all
\VThreeDirectTransitionCount{} adjacent
nonfallback transitions are sequence-consistent.  Maximum velocity and
acceleration reach their configured limits, so their minimum margins are
exactly zero rather than strictly positive; they pass the registered
\(\VThreeAuditTolerance\) audit tolerance.  Maximum internal jerk remains below the
configured jerk limit.  These counts describe the frozen direct
constant-jerk method label and do not attribute the observation to its PVA
target components.

The separately defined post-warm-up runtime population contains
\VThreeRuntimeCycleCount{} cycles (\cref{tab:v3-runtime}).  End-to-end runtime
has p99 \SI{\VThreeRuntimePNinetyNineUS}{\micro\second}, maximum
\SI{\VThreeRuntimeMaxUS}{\micro\second}, and zero recorded
\SI{\ControlPeriodMS}{\milli\second} deadline misses.  This is an empirical
distribution on one recorded software/hardware environment, not a
worst-case-execution-time statement.  Neither the safety counts nor the
runtime counts establish plant-dynamics, hardware, collision, or production
safety, and zero recorded events are not a universal failure-probability
claim.

\subsection{Profile-aware auditing changes what can be claimed}

% CLAIM: C11,C12
Current profile-aware infrastructure represents an exposed native
\Ruckig{} control-period prefix as ordered piecewise-constant-jerk segments
and integrates those segments individually.  If an exact internal segment
model is unavailable, the record marks internal jerk unavailable rather than
substituting average acceleration-difference jerk.  The same records separate
native \OrdinaryRuckig{}, \ViabilityShieldedRuckig{}, and
\DirectConstantJerkExecution{}, including any algorithm-changing fallback.
This makes method semantics auditable in current code.  It is not a v3
rerun, a corrected comparative outcome, or evidence that one follower
outperforms another.
